Project Presentation

Does equity toxic-flow detection survive the move to crypto?

Adverse-selection (“toxic”) flow is a market maker’s core risk. Equity microstructure detects it with tools like VPIN and widens spreads in response. I test whether those tools still work on anonymous, high-frequency crypto perpetual futures.

The venue has no counterparty identity and no queue position — the exact inputs these tools assume. So does the signal transfer, or break?
Framing
A methodological transfer study — a pipeline and rigorous evaluation, not a trading product.
Data
Bybit BTC / ETH / SOL perps across three regimes: calm, breakout, stress correction.
Result
VPIN ≈ random (AUC 0.506); a simple classifier beats it (AP 0.29 vs 0.18) under stress.

Reflection

What’s still open, and what’s next

Fix

Still open

Merge robustness. The trade/book merge is positional with a row-count guard; a timestamp-keyed join removes the silent drift risk properly.
Historical results not rerun. The PnL table and confidence intervals predate the causal fix — they’re a historical, oracle-signal result, not evidence of classifier-driven improvement yet.
Statistical power. ~7 daily blocks per week — confidence intervals span zero in most cells. Needs months of untouched out-of-sample data, not three selected weeks.

Add

Toward production

Event-time streaming. Replace batch replay with live ingestion and an incrementally maintained book, instead of a full re-sort per trade.
Real transaction costs. Queue position, fees, and rebates — the current fill model is optimistic about what actually gets filled and at what price.
Longer untouched data. Months of live data, not a hand-selected three-week sample across three regimes.
Takeaway: the value here isn’t a profitable strategy claim — it’s the discipline of finding out exactly where a pipeline like this breaks, and being honest about what’s still unverified.
